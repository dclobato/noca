How a submission becomes a verdict

Contest and Arena never execute submitted code themselves. They durably record the submission, then hand the work to AutoJudge across an infrastructure boundary.

What runs behind them

These are part of this environment but have no page to visit. They do their work on queues and timers, and you meet them through Contest and Arena.

AutoJudge
Compiles and runs every submission from both products, in isolated containers with enforced time, memory, process, and output limits.
Rating
Recomputes Arena problem difficulty, user and affiliation ratings, statistics, and badges on a schedule. Optional, and single-replica by design.
AI Assistant
Reviews an Arena submission on request and explains where it goes wrong without writing the solution. Optional; Arena works fully without it.
Mailer
Delivers every email Contest and Arena send — credentials, confirmations, class notices — from a queue, at a steady pace, so no page ever waits on a mail server. Required: Contest and Arena wait for it at startup.
Shared services
The database schema, language registry, queue payloads, and scoring rules both products agree on. No runtime imports another's code.

Which door do you want?

Contest and Arena keep separate accounts. Registering for one does not sign you in to the other.

Contest

For organizers, judges, staff, and competing teams — before, during, and after an event.

  • Multiple concurrent contests, each with isolated users, problems, schedules, and scoreboards.
  • Roles for administrators, judges, staff, teams, and read-only observers, scoped to one contest.
  • Automatic judging or a human-confirmation workflow, with rejudging and chief-judge overrides.
  • Live, frozen, and final scoreboards, plus reports and multi-site organization.
  • Clarification, balloon, print, and SOS task queues for the event floor.
  • Problem packages, PDF and Markdown statements, and Auto-Limit profiling of reference solutions.

Judged against every active language in the shared registry, which currently defines 21 languages.

Arena

For learners and teachers who want the judge available on an ordinary Tuesday, not only on competition day.

  • Free self-service registration, email confirmation, and optional two-factor authentication.
  • Teacher-owned classes and scheduled problem sets, with reviewed registration requests.
  • Public problem browsing with samples, statistics, and rating history.
  • Interactive problems with custom validators, marked as such before you open them.
  • Profiles, leaderboards, live submission activity, and Capybara badges.
  • AI review that explains where a submission goes wrong instead of writing the solution for you, using your own OpenAI key or platform-funded credits.
  • An age gate that declines registrations under 13 and asks for guardian consent from 13 to 17.

Ratings, statistics, badges, and AI review depend on optional workers. Submitting and judging never do.
