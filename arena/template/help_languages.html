Interactive problems (custom validators)

Some problems have no fixed expected output. Instead of comparing your output against a stored answer file, the problem author supplies a program — the custom validator — and the judge runs it side by side with your solution: everything your program prints is read by the validator, and everything the validator prints is fed back to your program's input. The two exchange messages until the conversation ends. Problem statements of such problems describe the protocol you must follow; any test cases they show are illustrative samples only, and the judge does not use them.

When the conversation ends, the validator decides the outcome of your attempt: whether your answers were correct, wrong, badly formatted, or whether you took too many queries or too much time for the budget the problem allows. The remaining verdicts still come from the judging environment itself, which enforces compilation, memory, output and process limits and detects a crashing program exactly as it does on regular problems. A crash on your side always wins over whatever the validator was about to report.

{{ render_icon(icon="warning", classes="flex-shrink-0 mt-1") }}
Because your program and the validator take turns waiting for each other, you must flush your output after every message you send (for the exact syntax for your language, see the stdout flush detail in the Available languages tab). A buffered message that never reaches the validator deadlocks the attempt.
